# Sources and originality

## Intellectual boundary

Rival Signal is independently written software. Its structure, interface, wording, research prompt, review rules, map formulas as implemented, fictional example, documentation and visual identity were created for this repository.

It does not reproduce lecture slides, speaker notes, classroom cases, assignments, exam material, course diagrams or institution-specific teaching material. General topics such as competitor analysis, competitive dynamics and response planning only define the problem domain. The implementation rests on public publications.

The demo case (FjordDesk, RoutePilot, SuiteWorks and the spreadsheet-and-phone substitute), its criteria, evidence, sources at `rival-demo.example` and review decisions are generated by code and wholly fictional. They are not market evidence and describe no real business.

## What the sources support

The same list ships inside the app (**Research & limits**), in the printable brief and in every evidence pack.

- **Chen (1996)** integrates competitor analysis and interfirm rivalry through two firm-specific constructs, market commonality and resource similarity, seen from a focal firm. Rival Signal keeps market overlap and capability resemblance as separate axes, relative to the focal company. Its weighted yes/no coding is an application-defined proxy, not a replication of Chen's measures or a validated threat score.
- **Bergen and Peteraf (2002)** broaden competitor identification through customer needs and resource equivalence, which brings indirect and potential competitors into view. Rival Signal asks for indirect rivals, potential entrants and substitutes in the brief, and lets a manual workaround be a rival.
- **Chen, Su and Tsai (2007)** examine competitive tension through the awareness–motivation–capability perspective. Rival Signal uses awareness, motivation and capability as separate prompts when writing a response hypothesis. It does not estimate the study's model or turn the prompts into probabilities.
- **Montgomery, Moore and Urbany (2005)** report, across three exploratory studies, limited strategic reasoning by executives about competitors' reactions, alongside difficulty obtaining information and uncertainty about behaviour. Rival Signal asks for competing hypotheses, counterevidence and observable signals before a reaction is chosen. That is a reasoning aid, not evidence that it improves forecasts.
- **Jaźwińska and Chandrasekar (2025)**, a Tow Center report in the *Columbia Journalism Review*, found citation and attribution errors when generative search tools were asked to identify news articles. It is not peer-reviewed and not about competitor research. Rival Signal takes from it only the practical point that AI-sourced research needs a source-alignment check before it counts.

No source validates the app's coding rules, weights, 180-day default age limit, bounds or public-demo caps. Those are transparent design choices.
